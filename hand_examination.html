1
Rapport Building & Communication
Permission · Introduction · Explanation
DOMAIN 1 ▼
📋 What to Say & Do
  • Greet and introduce yourself: "Good morning, I'm Dr. [name]. I'm going to examine your hands today."
  • Ask about background: "Have you had any hand examination before? Can you tell me a little about your complaint?"
  • Ask permission: "Is it alright if I begin the examination of your hands?"
  • Explain the steps: "I'll be looking at your hands, feeling for tenderness, checking your movements, strength, and sensation, then doing some special tests."
  • Ask patient to tell you if anything is uncomfortable at any point.
⭐ Exam Tip — High Yield
The examiner is watching for fluency and naturalness — don't just say words mechanically. Make genuine eye contact, use the patient's name, and explain each step AS you do it, not before.
2
Infection Control — ABHR
Alcohol Based Hand Rub · 6 steps · 30 seconds
CRITICAL ▼
🧠 Mnemonic — ABHR 6 Steps: "PP BBT T"
PP B B T T
1Palm to Palm — rub both palms together
2Palm over Back — right palm over dorsum of left, interlaced fingers; then swap
3Between Fingers — palm to palm with fingers interlaced
4Back of Fingers — backs of fingers against opposing palms (interlocked)
5Top / Nails — rotational rubbing of right thumb in left palm; then swap
6Thumb — rotational rubbing of closed right fingers on left palm; then swap
⚠️ Rules
  • Perform BEFORE touching the patient AND AFTER the examination
  • Minimum 30 seconds per hand rub
  • Both omissions = automatic fail on this domain
⭐ Exam Tip — High Yield
Say out loud: "I'm now applying alcohol-based hand rub before examining the patient." Then verbalize again at the end. Examiners cannot give marks for something they don't see OR hear.
3
Inspection — Both Hands
Skin · Nails · Swellings · Deformities · Muscle Wasting
LOOK ▼
👁️ What to Look For — Compare Both Sides
  • Skin: Erythema, pallor, bruising, scars, rashes, skin tightening (scleroderma)
  • Nails: Pitting (psoriasis), onycholysis, clubbing, koilonychia, nail-fold infarcts (vasculitis)
  • Swellings: DIP joints (Heberden's nodes – OA), PIP joints (Bouchard's nodes – OA; or RA), MCP joints (RA), wrist (ganglion, RA synovitis)
  • Deformities: Ulnar deviation (RA), Swan neck, Boutonnière, Z-deformity of thumb (RA), Dupuytren's contracture
  • Muscle wasting: Thenar eminence (median nerve — CTS), Hypothenar (ulnar nerve), Interosseous wasting (RA, ulnar nerve)
🎯 CTS-Specific Findings on Inspection
  • Thenar muscle wasting (late sign — severe/chronic CTS)
  • No visible swelling at wrist in most cases
  • Skin may be dry (autonomic involvement)
⭐ Exam Tip
Always start dorsum → then palmar surface. Always ask patient to turn hands over. Verbalize every finding (even normal): "I can see no skin changes, no nail abnormalities, no obvious deformities or swelling. I notice the thenar eminence appears slightly flattened on the right compared to the left."
4
Temperature Assessment
Forearms and Hands · Use Dorsum of Examiner's Hand
FEEL ▼
🌡️ Technique
  • Use the dorsum of your hand (more sensitive to temperature)
  • Start from forearm → move distally to the hand
  • Compare both sides simultaneously or sequentially
  • Verbalize: "I'm assessing the temperature of both forearms and hands…"
🔍 What Temperature Tells You
  • Warm + swollen joint → active inflammation (RA, gout, septic arthritis)
  • Cool hands → poor perfusion, Raynaud's, peripheral vascular disease
  • CTS → usually no temperature change (nerve compression)
⭐ Exam Tip
This step is often forgotten. Even if you find nothing abnormal, say: "Temperature is normal and equal in both hands." The examiner is scoring whether you did it, not what you found.
5
Palpation — Joints, Eminences & Radial Pulse
Tenderness · Swelling · Thenar & Hypothenar Bulk · Radial Pulse
High Yield ▼
✋ Palpation Sequence
  • 1. Finger joints: Palpate each DIP, PIP, MCP joint — squeeze gently from sides to assess tenderness and detect synovial thickening or effusion
  • 2. Wrist joint: Palpate with both thumbs on the dorsum — feel for boggy synovitis, bony tenderness, or crepitus
  • 3. Thenar eminence: Palpate at base of thumb (median nerve muscles — Opponens pollicis, Abductor pollicis brevis, Flexor pollicis brevis) — compare bulk with other side
  • 4. Hypothenar eminence: Palpate at base of little finger (ulnar nerve muscles)
  • 5. Radial pulse: Palpate radial pulse at the wrist — document rate and regularity
🎯 CTS: Expected Palpation Findings
  • Thenar wasting on right (late sign)
  • Tenderness over carpal tunnel (volar wrist crease, centre)
  • No joint swelling or synovitis
  • Radial pulse — normal
⭐ Exam Tip — High Yield
Always watch the patient's face when palpating for pain — not just your hands. Say: "Tell me if I press anywhere tender." Palpating radial pulse is a scored checkpoint — don't skip it.
6
Range of Movement — Passive & Active
Wrist · MCP · PIP · DIP · Pain · Crepitus
MOVE ▼
🔄 Sequence: Active First → Then Passive
  • Ask patient to perform movements first (active) — this tells you their pain and willingness
  • Then you gently move the joint through its range (passive) — feel for crepitus, end-feel, restriction
  • For each movement verbalize: "I'm checking [joint] flexion/extension… any pain? Any clicking?"

⭐ Exam Tip
In CTS, wrist range of movement is usually normal. The finding to report: "Full range of movement in all joints. No pain or crepitus." Then proceed to the power and sensation assessment.
7
Power & Sensation
Power Grip · Pincer Grip · Light Touch · Pin Prick · Compare Both Sides
High Yield ▼
💪 Power Assessment
  • Power grip: Ask patient to squeeze your 2 fingers as tightly as possible — both hands simultaneously or separately
  • Pincer grip (key pinch): Thumb against index finger — resist separation. "Hold this as tight as you can, don't let me pull it apart."
  • Thumb abduction: Ask patient to point thumb to ceiling and resist downward pressure — tests APB (median nerve / CTS)
  • Grade using MRC scale 0–5
🎯 Sensation — Median Nerve Territory
  • Use cotton wool (light touch) and/or pin prick
  • Median nerve: Palmar surface of thumb, index, middle finger, and lateral ½ of ring finger
  • Ulnar nerve: Little finger and medial ½ of ring finger
  • Compare right vs left — ask: "Does this feel the same on both sides? Is it sharper, duller, or the same?"
  • In CTS: reduced sensation in median nerve territory — especially index + middle finger tips
⭐ Exam Tip — High Yield
Always compare both sides. Start with the normal (left) side so the patient knows what "normal" feels like. In this patient: sensation reduced in lateral 2/3 of right palm (thumb/index/middle anteriorly). Medial 1/3 also slightly reduced.
8
Provocative Tests — Phalen's, Tinel's, Durkan's
Carpal Tunnel Syndrome Confirmation Tests
CRITICAL ▼
🧠 Mnemonic — "Please Try Driving" = PTD
P T D
PPhalen's — wrist flexion test
TTinel's — tapping test
DDurkan's — direct compression test (most sensitive)
Test Technique Positive Result Notes
Phalen's Both wrists fully flexed (dorsum-to-dorsum) for 60 seconds Tingling/numbness in median nerve territory (thumb, index, middle) Sensitivity ~68%, Specificity ~73%
Tinel's Sign Tap with finger over the carpal tunnel (volar wrist crease, centre) Tingling radiates distally into thumb/index/middle Sensitivity ~50%, Specificity ~77%
Durkan's (Carpal Compression) Apply direct firm pressure over carpal tunnel with both thumbs for 30 seconds Tingling/numbness in median nerve territory Most sensitive — Sensitivity ~87%, Specificity ~90%
⭐ Exam Tip — High Yield
In this OSCE station: the patient (simulated) will report tingling in thumb/index/middle with Phalen's, and tingling in lateral 2/3 of palm with Tinel's/Durkan's. This confirms CTS. Make sure to time 60 seconds for Phalen's and 30 seconds for Durkan's — do NOT rush.
9
Closing — Share Outcome & Thank Patient
Summary · Follow-up · Questions
DOMAIN 3 ▼
✅ What to Say
  • Share findings: "I've finished the examination. I found some reduction in sensation in your right hand, particularly in the thumb, index, and middle fingers, and a slight reduction in the power of your right thumb. The special tests I performed suggest possible compression of the median nerve at the wrist — this is called carpal tunnel syndrome."
  • Explain next steps: "I'd like to arrange some nerve conduction studies to confirm this. We'll then discuss treatment options, which can include splinting, injections, or a minor surgical procedure."
  • ABHR again after contact
  • Thank the patient: "Thank you for coming in today. Do you have any questions for me?"
⭐ Exam Tip
Don't forget to do ABHR at the END. Then finish with: "Do you have any questions?" — this is a scored item on the mark sheet (Closing the session). A warm, caring closing tone matters for the communication domain score.
