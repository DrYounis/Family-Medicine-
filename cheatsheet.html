Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP)

📐 Examination Quick Facts

  • Patient angle45° reclined; head 10–20° to left
  • Reference pointSternal angle (angle of Louis) + 5 cm → CVP
  • Normal JVP6–8 cm H₂O (1–3 cm above sternal angle)
  • <5 cmHypovolaemia
  • >9 cmElevated — impaired cardiac filling
  • Vein usedInternal jugular (preferred) or external jugular
  • JVP vs CarotidJVP = biphasic, non-palpable, obliterated by pressure, decreases with inspiration

〰️ JVP Waveform

Mnemonic — "a c x v y"
acxvy
aAtrial contraction → ends with carotid pulse
ctriCuspid valve bulges toward RA during RV contraction
xatrial relaXation + RV pulls TV down (x' = RV contractility)
vVenous filling — TV closed, blood builds up
yrapid emptYing when TV opens

🔴 6 Causes of Elevated JVP

Mnemonic — "RF TC SA"
RFTCSA
RRight heart failure — most common; often 2° to LHF
FFluid overload — excess intake or renal failure
TTricuspid regurgitation — massive v wave; no x descent
CComplete heart block — AV dissociation → cannon a waves
SSVC obstruction — elevated WITHOUT pulsation; HJR negative
AAtrial fibrillation — no atrial systole → absent a wave

Cardiology

🫀 Cor Pulmonale

  • DefinitionRV failure caused by pulmonary disease (not primary cardiac)
  • Classic caseHeavy smoker + SOB + foot oedema + no LHF history
  • ECGRight axis deviation + RBBB
  • EchoNormal LV; hypertrophied RV; paradoxical IVS bulging
  • CauseCOPD most common; also PE, pulm. fibrosis, OSA

💊 Hypertension — Drug Choice (ACC/AHA)

Mnemonic — A B C D
ABCD
AACE inhibitor / ARB — DM, CKD, proteinuria, heart failure
BBeta-blocker — post-MI, heart failure, angina, AF
CCalcium channel blocker — elderly, Afro-Caribbean
DDiuretic (thiazide) — elderly, Afro-Caribbean, isolated systolic
Normal labs + isolated HTN → Lisinopril/HCTZ 10/12.5 mg daily (ACC/AHA guideline)

🩺 Polymyalgia Rheumatica (PMR)

  • Age>50 yrs (usually >65)
  • SymptomsBilateral shoulder + hip girdle pain, morning stiffness >45 min, weight loss, fatigue
  • LabsESR >50 (often >100), CRP elevated; CK normal (unlike polymyositis)
  • TreatmentLow-dose Prednisolone — dramatic response confirms diagnosis
  • Vs. polymyositisPMR: no muscle weakness, CK normal. Polymyositis: weakness + ↑CK

Musculoskeletal

🦴 Scaphoid Fracture

  • MechanismFall on outstretched hand (FOOSH)
  • SignAnatomical snuffbox tenderness — pathognomonic
  • X-rayMay be normal initially (fracture not visible for 10–14 days)
  • ManagementThumb Spica cast + repeat X-ray in 2–3 weeks (treat as scaphoid Fx until proven otherwise)
  • ComplicationAvascular necrosis (blood supply enters distally)
Never say "no fracture, go home." Missed scaphoid = AVN.

🦵 Knee — Ottawa Rules (X-ray needed if…)

Mnemonic — "FIFE Walk"
FIFE
FAge ≥55 (or <18 with growth plates)
IIsolated patellar tenderness (no bony tenderness elsewhere)
FFibular head tenderness
EExtension — unable to flex to 90°; unable to walk 4 steps
Obese patient, fall on ice, can flex >90°, walks with limp → X-ray indicated (age ≥55)

Internal Medicine

🧪 Adrenal Insufficiency

  • PresentationFatigue, anorexia, nausea, hypotension, hyperpigmentation
  • ScreeningMorning cortisol level (8 AM)
  • Confirmatory testACTH stimulation test (Synacthen test)
  • If low cortisol response → primary AIAddison's disease

🫀 Primary Biliary Cholangitis (PBC)

  • WhoMiddle-aged women, fatigue, RUQ pain
  • ExamHepatomegaly, xanthomas, jaundice (late)
  • Labs↑↑ ALP, normal ALT/AST, positive AMA (anti-mitochondrial Ab)
  • TreatmentUrsodeoxycholic acid (UDCA)
ALP ↑ + AMA positive = PBC until proven otherwise

🫘 Nephrology Pearls

  • Low eGFR in bodybuilderHigh muscle mass → ↑ creatinine → falsely lowers eGFR (not true CKD)
  • ADPKD screeningUltrasound abdomen — first line, non-invasive
  • Elderly incontinence + bacteriuriaIf asymptomatic → NO antibiotics. Treat with scheduled voiding (immobility)

🩸 Haematology

  • Von Willebrand Type 1Mild; treat with Desmopressin (DDAVP) — releases vWF from endothelium
  • Von Willebrand Type 2/3Factor VIII + vWF concentrate (DDAVP not effective)
Dental extraction + Type 1 vWD → Desmopressin given before procedure

🔬 Other High-Yield Pearls

  • Ischemic colitisElderly + bloody diarrhea + severe LLQ pain
  • Smoking cessationMost effective: Varenicline + nicotine gum
  • Shingrix vaccine2 doses recombinant zoster vaccine (preferred even if had live vaccine >5 yrs ago)
  • Sea sting (punctuate lesions)Vinegar
  • Acoustic neuromaDizziness + unilateral hearing loss + tinnitus + facial numbness
Emergency Medicine

🚨 CO Poisoning

Management Mnemonic — "O-HOT"
OHOT
O100% Oxygen — first step ALWAYS
HHyperbaric O₂ — if severe (coma, cardiac, pregnant, carboxyHb >25%)
OObservation — monitor neurological status
TTransfer — remove from source immediately
Pregnant mother and child always need hyperbaric O₂ even if asymptomatic (fetal Hb binds CO more avidly)

⚡ Emergency Quick Hits

  • Fainting spells (arrhythmia?)First investigation: ECG
  • Peripheral vertigoHallpike positive — violent symptoms after latent period
  • Ventricular septal bulge + RHFCor pulmonale (paradoxical IVS)
  • Pancreatitis cause (radiating to back)Biliary tract disease (gallstones) #1
Levels of Prevention

🛡️ Prevention Levels

Mnemonic — "Please See The Quality"
PSTQ
PPrimary — before disease occurs (vaccines, lifestyle, health education, smoking cessation)
SSecondary — early detection (screening, BP check, mammography, Pap smear)
TTertiary — limit disability after disease established (cardiac rehab post-MI, physiotherapy, chemotherapy)
QQuaternary — prevent over-medicalization (avoid unnecessary treatments/investigations)
Cardiac rehab after MI = Tertiary prevention
